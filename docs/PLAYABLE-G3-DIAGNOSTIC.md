# Why the playable engine sounds like a struck bar

A listening note on the 0.1.2 playable engine, the one in the RackForge
plugin, said it sounds like a glockenspiel. This diagnostic measures that
impression against the retained G3 recordings with the existing analysis
tools, without changing any model. It is a measurement of the plugin's
engine, not of the laboratory assembly.

## Protocol

Three isolated G3 notes (MIDI 55) were rendered with the playable engine at
44.1 kHz, 8 s long with a 7.5 s hold, at velocities 1.0, 0.6 and 0.25, and
compared with the retained jRhodes layers 1, 3 and 5 respectively. Layer
levels are processed and uncalibrated, so absolute levels are not compared;
harmonic balance relative to the fundamental, component frequencies and
decay slopes are.

```text
cargo run --locked --release -p rf-tines-lab -- render --output g3-v1.0.wav --note 55 --velocity 1.0 --sample-rate 44100 --seconds 8 --hold 7.5
cargo run --locked --release -p rf-tines-lab -- compare-tone LAYER.wav g3-v1.0.wav --note 55 --output tone.json --reference-start 0 --candidate-start 0
cargo run --locked --release -p rf-tines-lab -- compare-partials LAYER.wav g3-v1.0.wav --output attack.json --seconds 1 --reference-start 0.02 --candidate-start 0.02 --partial-window-ms 128
cargo run --locked --release -p rf-tines-lab -- compare-partials LAYER.wav g3-v1.0.wav --output sustain.json --seconds 4 --reference-start 0.5 --candidate-start 0.5 --partial-window-ms 512
```

The nine comparison reports and three render receipts are retained under
`references/g3-playable-diagnostic/` with a manifest of sizes and SHA-256
hashes. The loud pair's partial tracking exceeded the tracker's capacity on
the recording side, so its matching is disabled and only its per-side tracks
and the tone comparison are read; the other two pairs tracked completely.

## What the recordings have and the engine lacks

### A harmonic series that grows with dynamics

Levels relative to each signal's own fundamental, 96 ms attack window:

| Harmonic | Real layer 1 (loud) | Engine at 1.0 | Real layer 3 | Engine at 0.6 | Real layer 5 (soft) | Engine at 0.25 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2 (393 Hz) | +2.4 dB | −3.3 dB | −17.4 dB | −10.6 dB | −21.7 dB | −21.6 dB |
| 3 (589 Hz) | +7.0 dB | −12.4 dB | −13.6 dB | −24.5 dB | −41.6 dB | −45.7 dB |
| 4 (786 Hz) | −9.2 dB | −32.8 dB | −17.3 dB | −56.2 dB | −55.9 dB | absent |
| 5 (982 Hz) | +1.8 dB | −32.9 dB | −24.6 dB | −57.6 dB | absent | absent |
| 6 (1179 Hz) | −0.1 dB | −54.0 dB | −34.5 dB | absent | absent | absent |

At the soft dynamic the engine's balance is within 4 dB of the recording
through the third harmonic. At the loud dynamic the recording's third and
fifth harmonics are louder than its fundamental and the sixth is level with
it, while the engine's fall 19 to 54 dB short. The power centroid of the loud
attack is 715 Hz in the recording and 274 Hz in the engine. The engine's
loud note is not too bright; it is far too dark, and its brightness barely
moves with velocity. This is the missing pickup nonlinearity: the harmonic
series in a Rhodes is generated by the pickup from a nearly sinusoidal tine
motion, and it is what makes a loud note growl.

### Sustain that lasts four times longer

Fundamental decay slopes in the 0.5–4.5 s region:

| Pair | Real fundamental (dB/s) | Engine fundamental (dB/s) | Real 2nd harmonic | Engine 2nd harmonic |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Loud | −2.1 / −2.9 | −11.2 / −11.3 | +2.3 / −5.1 | −22.6 / −22.7 |
| Medium | −2.8 / −2.6 | −11.3 / −11.3 | −5.1 / −5.1 | −22.6 / −22.7 |
| Soft | −2.4 / −2.3 | −11.3 / −11.3 | −4.7 / −4.6 | −22.7 / −22.7 |

Early and late slopes are listed. The recording's fundamental decays at
about 2.5 dB/s, a T60 near 24 s; the engine's decays at 11.3 dB/s, a T60
near 5.3 s, at every velocity. The matched-track comparison puts the
engine's fundamental T60 17 to 21 s shorter than the recording's. The
recording's second harmonic decays at 5 dB/s and the engine's at 22.6 dB/s,
so every overtone the engine does produce is gone within a second. The
laboratory's conditional sustain calibration already selected a 30 s first
tine T60 for the loaded assembly; the playable engine never received it.

### The bar partial is there, but wrong and brief

Revised by the [bar partial block](PLAYABLE-BAR-PARTIAL.md): the recording's
line at six times the fundamental behaves as the pickup's sixth harmonic, not
as a bending partial, and the engine's second partial is over-excited rather
than too brief. The paragraph below is kept as written at the time.

The recording carries the tine's second bending partial at 1178 to 1179 Hz,
6.0 times the fundamental, at −9.7 dB in the loud attack and audible through
the whole 4 s sustain window at the medium dynamic. The engine's second
partial sits at 1228 Hz, 6.27 times the fundamental and 72 cents sharp, at
−44 to −47 dB, and disappears within 130 ms at every velocity. The 6.27
ratio is the uniform clamped bar's; the real tine's tuning weight lowers it
to 6.0 and the recording keeps it ringing. The laboratory's modal
preparation with the movable tuning mass already predicts this shift; the
playable engine uses the fixed uniform ratios.

## Reading

The glockenspiel impression is a vibraphone-like signature: a fundamental
that is nearly pure within a second, a brief faint inharmonic ping at the
attack, and a decay four times too fast. A Rhodes instead keeps a harmonic
series that the pickup generates and that grows with velocity, a fundamental
that rings for twenty seconds, and a sustained second bar partial at six
times the fundamental. None of these is an action property; all three are
resonator, loss and pickup properties that the laboratory has already
studied and that the playable engine has never received.

For the reduced real-time engine this fixes the order of work: first the
pickup transfer and its velocity-dependent harmonic generation, second the
per-partial losses with the calibrated first-mode T60 and a slow second
harmonic, third the tuned second partial at 6.0 times the fundamental with
its own sustain. The baked action tables come after, because they change how
the note is reached, not what it sounds like once reached.

## Scope

Three velocities, one note, processed recordings with unknown capture gain
and a tracker that saturated on the loud recording. Harmonic balance and
decay slopes are diagnostic measurements, not calibration targets, and the
recordings' EQ and noise reduction affect weak components. A receipt test
holds the manifest hashes, the fundamental frequency agreement, the
fundamental decay slopes on both sides and the loud third-harmonic deficit.
